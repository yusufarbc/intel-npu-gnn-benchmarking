## Executive Summary

The deployment of deep learning models on Neural Processing Units (NPUs) is fundamentally constrained by the **Memory Wall**—the growing disparity between compute throughput and memory bandwidth . While operator fusion remains the primary compiler-level strategy to mitigate this bottleneck, recent empirical research reveals a **Fusion Overhead Paradox**: the compilation and dispatch overhead of fusion can exceed execution benefits for small models, and the lack of native NPU operator support forces costly CPU fallbacks that fragment execution pipelines .

Contemporary frameworks (ONNX Runtime, OpenVINO) exhibit **super-linear compilation times** (58–62 seconds for 8B-parameter models) and provide **no pass-level visibility**, preventing principled optimization . The FORGE-UGC compiler demonstrates that transparent, composable optimization passes can achieve **6.9–9.2× faster compilation** and **18.2–35.7% lower inference latency** through formal buffer management and device-affinity scheduling . However, the literature lacks systematic studies on sub-100M parameter models (e.g., BERT-tiny) where fusion overhead may dominate, and cross-device telemetry remains inadequate for edge deployment scenarios.

## Thematic Synthesis

### Theme 1: The Evolution of the Memory Wall

The shift from compute-bound to memory-bound AI workloads is well-documented through the Roofline model framework. Williams et al.'s original model has been adapted for NPU analysis, where **arithmetic intensity** (FLOPs/Byte) determines whether a workload is memory-bound or compute-bound .

For decoder-only LLMs, the operational intensity during autoregressive decoding approximates **OI ≈ 2/b_prec**, yielding approximately **1 FLOP/Byte for FP16 precision**—far below the ridge point of modern accelerators . This places virtually all edge LLM inference deep in the memory-bound regime. Gholami et al. (2024) quantified the exponential gap: compute performance grows at **3.0× per two years**, while DRAM bandwidth grows at only **1.6×** .

On edge devices specifically, RooflineBench (2026) reveals a **non-monotonic "arch" trajectory** in operational intensity as model depth increases . Initially, deeper models amortize fixed overheads (kernel launch, synchronization), shifting operational intensity rightward. However, beyond **3–5 layers**, the cumulative memory bandwidth pressure of streaming weights triggers a **regression in operational intensity**—the "memory wall" is encountered significantly earlier than theoretical predictions suggest . This finding is critical for NPU deployment: edge accelerators hit bandwidth limits at remarkably shallow depths.

The HBM3E bottleneck analysis confirms that **compute-bandwidth mismatch** (Roofline imbalance) is cited in **90% of surveyed patents** as the primary bottleneck category, followed by irregular KV-cache access (75%) and capacity constraints (60%) . Processing-in-Memory (PIM) emerges as the dominant architectural response, with Samsung, SK Hynix, and KAIST leading hardware-level innovations .

**Key Insight**: The Memory Wall is not merely a theoretical constraint but an empirical reality that manifests at **shallower model depths than expected** on edge NPUs, making compiler-level memory traffic reduction (via fusion) essential yet increasingly difficult to achieve without hardware-software co-design.

### Theme 2: Efficacy of Operator Fusion

Operator fusion reduces memory traffic by eliminating intermediate DRAM writes, but its efficacy is **highly dependent on model scale and architecture**.

#### When Fusion Works: Large CNNs and Deep Transformers

FORGE-UGC's empirical evaluation across six model families (125M–8B parameters) demonstrates that **attention fusion** is the single most critical optimization, delivering **16.6–29.6% wall-clock latency reduction** . The benefit scales with model depth: 12-layer GPT-2 gains 16.6%, while 32-layer Llama-3.1-8B gains 29.6% . This scaling occurs because deeper models contain more attention blocks that can be fused, amortizing the fixed overhead of fusion pattern matching.

For CNNs, the unified fusion pipeline (2025) shows that fusing convolution-batch normalization-ReLU chains reduces memory traffic proportionally to the number of eliminated intermediate tensors . The Neptune fusion system (2026) achieves significant speedups for sequence lengths >8192 by fusing reductions into single kernels, avoiding kernel launch overhead and data movement .

#### When Fusion Fails or Adds Overhead: Small Models and Unsupported Operators

The **Fusion Overhead Paradox** manifests in several empirical scenarios:

1. **Compilation Time Dominance**: FORGE-UGC's per-pass profiling reveals that **operator fusion is the most expensive pass** (72ms for GPT-2 125M), accounting for **34.6% of total optimization time** yet providing only modest node reduction (−12 nodes vs. −59 for attention fusion) . While this is negligible for large models, for sub-100M parameter models (e.g., BERT-tiny, MobileNet-v3), the compilation overhead may exceed the inference speedup, particularly in just-in-time deployment scenarios.

2. **Small Sequence Lengths**: Neptune's evaluation shows that for L2 norm and RMSNorm with M ≤ 8192, fusion provides only **marginal benefit** because PyTorch already generates single kernels without deep fusion; the overhead of fusion pattern matching yields no latency reduction .

3. **Model-Specific Limitations**: ONNX Runtime's troubleshooting documentation explicitly notes that graph optimizations may fail to apply when initializers appear in graph inputs, preventing constant folding and operator fusion . This opacity—combined with no pass-level visibility—makes debugging impossible .

4. **Quantization Ordering Sensitivity**: TVM's pattern engine eliminates **12% of graph nodes** when INT8 conversion precedes fusion, versus only **4%** when performed after schedule search, demonstrating that fusion efficacy depends on pass ordering .

**Critical Gap**: No published study systematically measures the **break-even point** where fusion compilation overhead exceeds inference benefits for small models (<100M parameters) on NPUs. FORGE-UGC's Compilation Efficiency Index (CEI) provides a framework (speedup per second of compile time), but empirical validation on tiny models remains absent .

### Theme 3: Compiler-Hardware Friction

The friction between compiler optimizations and NPU hardware capabilities creates **execution bubbles** that dominate end-to-end latency.

#### CPU Fallbacks and Execution Bubbles

Edge NPUs support a **narrow operator subset** (primarily INT8/FP16 matrix multiplication), forcing unsupported operations (softmax, normalization, tensor reshaping) to execute on CPU . This creates three penalties:

1. **Host-Device Synchronization**: CPU fallback introduces **execution bubbles** where the NPU idles while control returns to the host . On Jetson Orin, CPU-side launch overhead can **exceed kernel execution time**, accounting for **30–60% of end-to-end latency** during autoregressive decoding .

2. **Cross-Device Migration Penalty**: Moving tensors between CPU and NPU adds **4–15ms latency**, often offsetting hardware acceleration benefits . FORGE-UGC addresses this through device-affinity scheduling, reducing NPU↔CPU transitions by **42–65%** .

3. **Operator Coverage Variability**: The μNPU benchmark (2025) found that the NXP-MCXN947's eIQ Neutron NPU lacks native softmax support, necessitating CPU post-processing; the MAX78000 supports only restricted convolution kernel sizes (1×1, 3×3) . Unsupported operations incur **latency penalties** that are rarely quantified in framework benchmarks.

#### Super-Linear Compilation Time

OpenVINO and ONNX Runtime exhibit **compilation times scaling super-linearly with model depth** (T ∝ L^1.4), reaching **58–62 seconds for 8B-parameter models** . This is prohibitive for iterative development and just-in-time deployment. FORGE-UGC achieves linear scaling (T ≈ 210·L ms) through composable O(|V|+|E|) passes versus monolithic O(|V|²) pattern matching .

#### Telemetry and Observability Gaps

Current frameworks lack **pass-level profiling** and **runtime telemetry** for NPU execution. ONNX Runtime provides a C++ profiling API that timestamps kernel launches and memory copies, but this requires manual instrumentation and lacks NPU-specific metrics . FORGE-UGC introduces per-pass execution profiling and the Fusion Gain Ratio (FGR) as a hardware-independent diagnostic, but FGR is **not calibrated to wall-clock latency** .

**Empirical Evidence**: A GitHub issue documents an **85-second compilation delay** in OpenVINO's VPUIP stage for Florence-2 vision encoder, with no workaround available . Another report shows that ORT 1.23.2 with OpenVINO 2025.3 **degraded latency** compared to direct OpenVINO calls, suggesting handover overhead between runtime and execution provider .

## Research Gaps

Based on this synthesis, the following critical gaps emerge in the literature:

1. **Dynamic Roofline Modeling for NPUs**: Existing Roofline analyses use static hardware ceilings. There is no empirical framework that dynamically models how compiler optimizations (fusion, quantization) shift the operational intensity of a model *during* compilation, particularly for heterogeneous execution (NPU+CPU).

2. **Sub-100M Parameter Model Overhead Analysis**: No study systematically quantifies the **break-even point** where fusion compilation overhead exceeds inference benefits for tiny models (BERT-tiny, DistilBERT, MobileNet-v3). FORGE-UGC's CEI metric provides a theoretical framework, but empirical validation on sub-100M models is absent .

3. **CPU Fallback Cost Quantification**: While the existence of execution bubbles is documented , there is no empirical model that predicts **per-operator fallback penalties** across different NPU architectures. This prevents compilers from making informed device-placement decisions.

4. **NPU-Specific Telemetry Tools**: Current observability stacks (OpenTelemetry, TVM runtime hooks) are designed for cloud/server deployment . Edge NPUs lack standardized telemetry for **per-instruction energy attribution**, **DMA transfer latency**, and **thermal throttling impact** on inference—critical for SLA-bound deployments.

5. **Pass-Level Autotuning**: OpenVINO's hint system (LATENCY/THROUGHPUT) is coarse-grained; ONNX Runtime's EP selection is static . There is no empirical study of **online pass ordering optimization** based on runtime feedback, nor of Bayesian optimization for NPU compilation configurations.

6. **Cross-Framework Compilation Time Benchmarking**: FORGE-UGC compares against OpenVINO and ONNX Runtime , but there is no standardized benchmark (analogous to MLPerf) for **compilation time vs. model depth** across frameworks, making it impossible to verify super-linear scaling claims independently.

7. **Precision-Aware Fusion Efficacy**: While quantization reduces memory traffic, its interaction with fusion is complex (TVM's 12% vs. 4% node elimination) . There is no empirical study of how **mixed-precision fusion** (FP16/INT8/INT4) affects NPU operational intensity and compiler overhead.

---

## Conclusion

The empirical literature confirms that the Memory Wall is the dominant constraint in NPU inference, and operator fusion is the primary compiler-level mitigation. However, the **Fusion Overhead Paradox**—where compilation and dispatch costs exceed benefits for small models—remains understudied. The lack of native operator support in edge NPUs creates **execution bubbles** that current compilers cannot adequately optimize, and the absence of pass-level telemetry prevents principled debugging. FORGE-UGC represents a significant advance in transparent compilation, but its validation is limited to Intel NPUs and models >100M parameters. Future research must address the sub-100M regime, standardized NPU telemetry, and dynamic Roofline-aware compilation to fully realize the potential of edge AI deployment.
