**Executive Summary**

In modern AI accelerators, particularly **Neural Processing Units (NPUs)** deployed in edge and client systems (e.g., Intel Core Ultra series), the dominant performance bottleneck has shifted decisively from arithmetic computation to **memory I/O and data movement**—the so-called "Memory Wall." Traditional RAM-model analysis, which assumes uniform and low-cost memory access, inadequately captures the realities of NPU execution, where off-chip DRAM bandwidth, on-chip buffer hierarchies, and frequent host-device (CPU-NPU) transitions dominate latency, energy, and throughput.

**Operator fusion**—a core graph optimization in compilers such as **ONNX Runtime (ORT)** and **OpenVINO**—serves as a primary mitigation strategy. It merges sequences of operators (e.g., Conv + BatchNorm + ReLU, or linear projection + activation) into single kernels, thereby reducing intermediate tensor materialization, kernel launch overhead, memory traffic, and dispatch costs. Empirical studies show substantial gains for high-arithmetic-intensity workloads like large CNNs, where fusion improves cache reuse and approaches the roofline's compute bound. However, fusion exhibits a "**Fusion Overhead Paradox**" in small models (e.g., BERT-tiny or lightweight transformers): compilation/dispatch overhead and enlarged temporary buffers can exceed benefits, especially when unsupported operators trigger costly **CPU fallbacks**. These fallbacks introduce serialization, additional data copies, and power spikes, eroding NPU advantages.

Recent literature (2021–2026) emphasizes hardware-software co-design, roofline-based modeling tailored to NPUs (accounting for multi-precision units, hierarchical buffers, and DMA), and empirical telemetry (latency, peak memory, power via RAPL or similar). While fusion and graph simplifications deliver 20–40%+ latency reductions in favorable cases, limitations in NPU operator coverage, opaque compiler pipelines, and static optimization heuristics leave significant headroom. Research increasingly highlights the need for dynamic, telemetry-driven optimizations and better visibility into NPU-specific memory behaviors.

**Thematic Synthesis**

**Theme 1: The Evolution of the Memory Wall — Shift from Compute-Bound to Memory-Bound AI Workloads**

Early deep learning accelerators focused on peak TOPS via systolic arrays or tensor cores, but post-2021 analyses document a clear transition: most inference workloads, especially autoregressive decoding in transformers and edge deployments, are memory-bound due to low arithmetic intensity (Ops/Byte). The classic **roofline model** (plotting performance against operational intensity bounded by peak compute and memory bandwidth) has been extended for NPUs to incorporate hierarchical memory (on-chip SRAM vs. off-chip DRAM), multi-precision datapaths, and DMA overheads.

Papers describe how data movement energy and latency now dwarf MAC operations. Techniques like **compute-in-memory (CiM)** or near-memory computing are proposed as architectural remedies, but in commercial NPUs, software mitigations dominate: tiling, data layout transformations, quantization, and fusion to maximize reuse within limited on-chip buffers. For NPUs, effective memory ceilings are often far below theoretical bandwidth due to contention, partial operator support, and host-device synchronization. Empirical roofline studies on platforms like Huawei Ascend or Intel NPUs reveal severe under-utilization (<30% of compute roof for many operators), with quadratic attention variants particularly penalized.

This shift underscores that compiler optimizations must prioritize I/O complexity reduction over pure FLOPs minimization, aligning with observations that batch size, precision, and model scale heavily influence whether a workload saturates memory or compute bounds.

**Theme 2: Efficacy of Operator Fusion — Success in Large CNNs vs. Overhead in Tiny Models and Unsupported Operators**

**Operator fusion** consistently improves performance when it eliminates memory-bound intermediates and kernel launches. In large CNNs or high-intensity layers (e.g., Conv2D + activations), fusion reduces L1/L2 traffic, lowers instruction cache pressure, and can yield 30–43% latency reductions on edge CPUs/GPUs, with analogous or greater gains on NPUs via fused intrinsics. Multi-level fusion (loop, kernel, operator, subgraph) further amplifies benefits in models with repetitive patterns like ResNet or transformers (e.g., attention fusion collapsing multi-head subgraphs).

However, efficacy diminishes or reverses in **small/tiny models** (BERT-tiny, MobileNet variants on edge NPUs). Here, fusion can enlarge ephemeral buffers, increase compilation time, or trigger suboptimal scheduling. The "Fusion Overhead Paradox" manifests as dispatch and planning costs outweighing execution savings, particularly under low batch sizes or variable shapes common in edge inference. Unsupported operators (e.g., certain Floor, int64 inputs, or custom attention) force partial graph execution on CPU, incurring data copies and serialization that negate NPU acceleration—sometimes making pure CPU execution faster for LLMs or VLMs on Intel NPUs.

Empirical telemetry shows fusion scales positively with model depth/size (e.g., greater gains on Llama-3.1-8B vs. GPT-2) but requires hardware-aware passes. Aggressive fusion in TVM-like compilers can bloat memory, while conservative approaches in ORT balance trade-offs. Recent work on NPU-specific compilers stresses pattern matching for native support and liveness analysis for buffer allocation to minimize transitions.

**Theme 3: Compiler-Hardware Friction — Overhead of CPU Fallbacks and Limitations of NPU Drivers/Compilers**

**OpenVINO** and **ONNX Runtime** (with OpenVINO EP) represent dominant stacks for Intel NPUs, performing graph-level optimizations (constant folding, dead-code elimination, layout transforms, fusion) during model import/IR conversion. Yet, friction arises from limited native operator coverage on NPUs: unsupported ops fallback to CPU via heterogeneous execution or reference implementations, introducing device transitions that spike power and latency.

OpenVINO's Model Optimizer and runtime plugin apply device-specific fusions, but pipelines often lack fine-grained visibility (e.g., no explicit user-controlled buffer allocation). ORT adds EP-specific extended fusions and memory planning, yet partitioning decisions can still yield suboptimal heterogeneous plans. For small models or dynamic shapes, compilation overhead and opaque heuristics exacerbate issues. Recent releases (e.g., OpenVINO 2026) improve NPU support for LLMs via MLP fusion, FP16 extensions, and memory sharing, but empirical reports highlight persistent gaps in telemetry and dynamic adaptation.

CPU fallbacks are particularly costly in memory-bound regimes, as they require explicit copies across host-device boundaries. Papers advocate composable, inspectable passes with ablation support and tighter integration (e.g., ahead-of-time NPU compilation) to reduce friction. Energy measurements (via RAPL) confirm that fewer transitions lower both latency and active power.

**Research Gaps**

Despite progress, several gaps persist in the 2021–2026 literature:

1. **Dynamic, workload-adaptive roofline modeling** for NPUs: Most extensions remain static or platform-specific; few incorporate runtime telemetry (e.g., real-time buffer pressure, DMA contention) or handle variable shapes/batches common in edge AI.

2. **Comprehensive telemetry tools** for end-to-end NPU inference: Opaque compiler pipelines (OpenVINO/ORT) limit ablation of individual passes (fusion vs. layout vs. scheduling). Lack of fine-grained power/latency breakdown per transition or buffer.

3. **Systematic quantification of the Fusion Overhead Paradox**: Few studies isolate compilation/dispatch costs vs. runtime savings across model scales, with explicit comparison of small/tiny models (BERT-tiny equivalents) under physical NPU constraints. CPU fallback overhead is acknowledged but rarely modeled with I/O complexity metrics.

4. **Heterogeneous execution modeling**: Better analytical or empirical frameworks for predicting partition quality and fallback penalties, especially as NPU operator support evolves.

5. **Longitudinal empirical studies** on evolving NPU generations (e.g., Intel Meteor Lake onward) with standardized benchmarks, focusing on memory-bound regimes rather than peak TOPS.

Addressing these would require open benchmarks with full telemetry exposure, more programmable NPU IRs, and hybrid analytical-empirical tools that bridge compiler graphs to physical hardware counters. Such advances are critical for Master's-level or industrial optimization of edge AI systems under strict memory and power envelopes.
